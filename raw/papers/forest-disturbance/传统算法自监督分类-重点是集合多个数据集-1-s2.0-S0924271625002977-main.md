The 100 km ×100 km study site has been actively harvested in recent decades and experienced many wildfires
and a mountain pine beetle (MPB) outbreak. The spatio-temporal accuracy of clearcut and fire-scar detection was
assessed using the Vegetation Resources Inventory (VRI) and National Burned Area Composite (NBAC) products,
respectively, and compared against the C2C 1985 – 2020, CCDC 2002 – 2019, and GFC 2001 – 2022 maps
available online. Overall, the UC–Change algorithm detected 85.2 % of the reference VRI 1974 – 2018 cutblock
pixels at a temporal resolution of ±1 year (90.3 % at ±3 years). It detected 86.0 % of 1985 – 2018 VRI pixels,
outperforming C2C (58.8 %). For the period 2002 – 2018, UC–Change mapped 87.1 % of the reference cutblock
pixels, exceeding C2C (54.5 %), CCDC (74.0 %), and GFC (70.4 %). UC–Change, C2C, CCDC, and GFC detected
71.0 %, 54.6 %, 37.4 %, and 67.2 % of 2006 – 2018 reference forest fire pixels, respectively. UC–Change pro-
vided improved forest harvest and fire-scar detection in areas heavily affected by the MPB outbreak and forests
characterized by low canopy cover. It represents a new, fundamentally different approach to time-series analysis,
suitable for independent use or in concert with existing methods.
